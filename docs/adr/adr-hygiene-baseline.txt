# ADR prose baselines — the ratchet for AdrCorpusParityTest.
#
# Four of the mechanical rules in docs/adr/TEMPLATE.md section 6 breach in bulk across a corpus written
# before they existed, so they cannot be a hard gate on day one. Each is a per-file NON-INCREASING
# RATCHET instead: this file records how many violations a record is allowed to carry, the gate FAILS
# when a record grows past its number, and it PRINTS (never fails) when a record improves, together
# with the exact replacement line. Improving prose must never break CI.
#
# The only direction a number may ever move is DOWN. Raising one to accommodate new prose defeats the
# whole mechanism; a record with no line here is allowed ZERO, which is what makes a new ADR clean.
#
# Format: "<rule> <adr-file> <count>", one per line. Blank lines and #-comments are ignored.
#   long-sentences        sentences over 60 words.
#   contractions          "can't", "isn't", "it's" — write them out.
#   semicolon-paragraphs  paragraphs carrying more than one semicolon.
#   long-lines            lines over 110 characters.
#
# Why 60 words and not 20 or 25: at 20 words 760 of the corpus's 1,773 sentences breach and at 25 words
# 554 do. Those thresholds describe a rewrite of every record, not a hygiene rule, and were rejected.
# 60 isolates the genuinely unreadable sentences (the worst is 117 words) and leaves ordinary technical
# prose alone.
#
# Why the semicolon rule is a ratchet and not a gate: 485 semicolons live in 50 of 50 records. A
# green-on-day-one semicolon rule does not exist for this corpus.
